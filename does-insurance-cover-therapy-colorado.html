Does Insurance Cover Therapy in Colorado?

When people hear a therapist is out-of-network, most assume that means paying the full fee forever. For a lot of Colorado plans, that isn't what happens. It's worth ten minutes on the phone before you decide therapy is out of reach.

In-network and out-of-network are not the same as covered and not covered

In-network means a therapist has a contract with your insurance company. The insurer sets the rate, and they also get a say in your diagnosis, how often you're seen, and when your care should end.

Out-of-network means no contract. You pay the therapist directly. Then, if your plan includes out-of-network benefits, you submit paperwork and your insurer reimburses part of what you paid.

Those are two different billing arrangements. Neither one tells you whether your plan will pay toward therapy.

What a superbill is

A superbill is an itemized receipt. It lists the dates you were seen, the service codes, the amount you paid, and my license and tax information. It's the document your insurer needs in order to process an out-of-network claim.

I prepare one monthly. You submit it through your insurer's app or member portal, usually by uploading a photo. There's no extra charge for it, and you don't have to ask.

Four questions that tell you what you actually have

Call the member services number on the back of your card and ask these, in this order:

  1. Do I have out-of-network outpatient mental health benefits?
  2. What is my out-of-network deductible, and how much of it have I met this year?
  3. Once the deductible is met, what percentage of the session fee is reimbursed?
  4. Do I need pre-authorization for outpatient mental health services?

Write down the answers and the date. Ask for a reference number for the call. Insurers make mistakes, and a reference number is what you use when someone tells you something different three months later.

What the math can look like

Say your plan has a $500 out-of-network deductible and reimburses 60% after that. Individual sessions here are $175.

The first three sessions are on you, and roughly cover the deductible. From session four on, you pay $175 and get about $105 back. Your real cost settles around $70 a session.

That's one example, not a promise. Deductibles run from zero to several thousand dollars and reimbursement rates swing widely. The point is that the sticker price and what you end up paying are often two different numbers, and you can't tell which you're dealing with until you ask.

If the answer is no

Some plans have no out-of-network benefits at all. Many HMO and Medicaid plans don't. That's a real answer and it's worth knowing early.

If that's where you land, say so when you reach out. I hold a limited number of reduced-fee slots, and I'd rather have the conversation about cost at the start than have you quietly decide not to call. Full details are on the fees page, including the Good Faith Estimate you're entitled to under the No Surprises Act.

Why I'm not in network

To bill insurance directly, I'd have to give you a diagnosis in the first session or two and keep justifying it. The insurer would have a say in how often we meet and when they think we should be finished.

Plenty of excellent therapists work inside that system and do good work there. I've chosen not to, because the kind of therapy I do often takes longer than a utilization reviewer thinks it should, and because what gets written in your permanent medical record matters.

If cost is the thing standing between you and starting, tell me. There is almost always more room than people assume.
