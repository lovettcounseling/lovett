When Someone Else Decided You Should Be Here

Not everyone picks up the phone because they want to. Sometimes it's a condition of something: a court, a probation officer, an HR file, a custody arrangement, a partner who said this was the last chance. If that's why you're reading this, you're not an unusual case. You're a sizable share of the people who ever walk into therapy.

Being resentful about it is a reasonable place to start

You were told to do a thing, on someone else's timeline, probably at your own expense. Showing up annoyed isn't a bad attitude. It's an accurate response to the situation.

I'd rather you say that out loud in the first ten minutes than spend six weeks performing enthusiasm neither of us believes.

What gets reported, and what doesn't

This is the question people actually want answered, and most are too guarded to ask it, so I'll answer it first.

If a third party requires documentation, what they're almost always entitled to is narrow: whether you attended, and sometimes whether you're participating. Not a transcript. Not what you said about your mother. Not the thing you're afraid will make it worse.

Anything beyond attendance requires a release that you sign, that names exactly who receives what, and that you can revoke. You read it before it goes anywhere. Nothing leaves this office that you haven't seen.

There are real limits to confidentiality, and they're the same for everyone: a credible risk to your life, a credible risk to someone else's, abuse or neglect of a child or an at-risk adult, or a court order I can't refuse. I'll go over those in the first session, in plain language, before you tell me anything.

Required and useless are not the same thing

Plenty of people arrive having already done a program that was a box to check. Sat in a circle. Read from a workbook. Signed a sheet. Learned nothing and resented the hours.

That experience is common enough that it's worth saying directly: this isn't that. The hour is yours. If what's most pressing is your landlord, or your sleep, or a thing from fifteen years ago that has nothing to do with why you were sent, that's what we work on.

You're paying for the time either way. You may as well get something out of it.

You can be honest without being reckless

People in this position often get very good at managing what they say. That skill was earned somewhere, usually for good reason, and I'm not going to ask you to drop it on day one.

What I will do is tell you the truth about where the edges are, so you can decide what you want to bring in. Informed is different from guarded. You can be careful and still be real.

Paperwork

If you need letters, attendance verification, or documentation on a set schedule, tell me at the start and I'll build it into how we work. I'd rather set it up correctly in week one than scramble the day before something is due.

There's no separate charge for routine documentation. Details are on the fees page.

If it turns out you want to keep going

It happens more than you'd think. The requirement ends and the person stays, because somewhere in the middle it stopped being about the requirement.

No pressure either way. But you're allowed to get something real out of something you didn't choose.
