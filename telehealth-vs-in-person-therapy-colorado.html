Telehealth or In-Person Therapy in Colorado? How to Choose

This comes up in almost every consultation call: "Is telehealth actually as good as in-person?" It's a fair question, and the honest answer is that it depends far more on your life and what you need than on the format itself. Here's how I'd actually think it through.

Both Are Real Therapy

Let's start here, because there's still a lingering assumption that telehealth is a lesser substitute. The research doesn't really support that for most concerns — outcomes for individual and couples therapy delivered over video are broadly comparable to in-person work for the majority of presenting issues. It's a different format, not a downgrade. The relationship and the work are still real; it's the room that's different.

When In-Person Might Serve You Better

Some people do better with in-person sessions, and it's worth being honest about why. If home doesn't feel private or safe enough for an open conversation, a therapy office solves that in a way a screen can't. Some people concentrate and settle into a session more easily away from the distractions of their own space. And for certain body-based or somatic work, being in the same room adds a layer that's harder to fully replicate remotely. None of these are wrong reasons to prefer in-person — they're just worth naming honestly instead of assuming in-person is the "correct" choice by default.

When Telehealth Might Serve You Better

For a lot of people, especially across Colorado outside the Denver metro area, telehealth isn't a compromise — it's what makes consistent therapy possible at all. No commute means no lost hour on either side of the session. It removes the geography question entirely, so you're not limited to whoever happens to practice within a reasonable drive. It travels with you, which matters if your schedule or location changes often. And for some people, being in their own space actually lowers the guard enough to go deeper, faster, than an unfamiliar office would.

What to Consider About Your Space and Routine

If you're leaning telehealth, it's worth being intentional about a few things: a space where you won't be interrupted or overheard, headphones if walls are thin, and a few minutes built in before and after the session instead of going straight from a work call into therapy and back. These sound minor, but they meaningfully change how much you get out of the hour.

It Doesn't Have to Be Permanent

This isn't a one-time, unchangeable decision. Plenty of clients start in one format and shift to the other as life changes — a move, a new job, a season where privacy at home gets easier or harder to come by. The honest answer to "which one should I pick" is usually: whichever one actually gets you into consistent sessions right now. That consistency matters more than the format.

If you're still not sure, that's exactly what the free 15-minute call is for — a chance to talk through your specific situation before deciding.
